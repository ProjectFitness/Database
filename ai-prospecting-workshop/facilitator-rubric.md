# Facilitator Guide and Rubric

Run of show for a 90-minute session. Times are elastic; the anonymous side-by-side is the one segment you must not cut.

## Agenda

| Time | Segment |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | Frame the session (5 min). Concede up front what AI fixed: formatting, volume, speed. "Nobody is taking your Gem away. We are talking about what it cannot do." |
| 0:05 | Round 1: reps draft with their normal AI workflow (10 min). Collect anonymously. |
| 0:15 | While reps take a break, paste all drafts into one doc, strip names, number them 1 through N. Highlight sentences that appear in near-identical form across multiple drafts. |
| 0:20 | Anonymous side-by-side reading (20 min). The discovery moment. Do not lecture during this segment. Ask, wait, let silence work. |
| 0:40 | Teach the Five Questions, hygiene checks, swap test, tiers (15 min). Use the eCapital before/after as the worked example. |
| 0:55 | Round 2: worksheet plus redraft (15 min). |
| 1:10 | Score 2 or 3 volunteer redrafts live against the rubric below, or run them through the Email Scorer prompt on screen (10 min). |
| 1:20 | Close: market math and career framing (10 min). Ask for one commitment: every rep runs their top-10 accounts through the Five Questions before their next send. |

## The anonymous side-by-side: mechanics and discussion questions

Mechanics: one shared screen, drafts numbered, identical or near-identical sentences pre-highlighted. Read two or three drafts aloud back to back.

Ask in this order, and make them answer before you comment:

1. "Whose draft is number 3? Don't tell me. Could anyone here prove it's theirs?" (If authorship is unprovable, the email carries no rep, and no relationship, in it.)
2. "How many of these sentences also work for the Public Storage / National Storage deal from March? Read yours and swap the names." (Live swap test on their own words.)
3. "Every BDR at every competitor has access to the same headline and the same AI. What is the market price of an email all of them can generate in 30 seconds?" (Let a rep say "zero" or "nothing". Do not say it for them.)
4. "Which draft would Benjamin Schall's chief of staff forward, and what sentence earns the forward?" (Shifts the frame from sender metrics to reader value.)
5. Only after all that: "What did the Gem actually do well here?" (Concede it again, out loud, so the exercise reads as market analysis, not a gotcha.)

## Scoring grid for Round 2 drafts

Score each redraft pass/fail per row. This mirrors the Email Scorer prompt, so reps can self-grade later with the same standard.

| Criterion | Pass looks like | Fail looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 Event | "announced May 21", "merger of equals" with date | "one of the largest multifamily REITs" (description) |
| Q2 Operational force | Two property management stacks, two resident platforms, two data environments becoming one while operating 180,000 units | Names the merger but jumps straight to pitching Symposium |
| Q3 Hard or routine | Signals the rep checked integration history of either company | No evidence the rep asked whether this is a first |
| Q4 Instance specificity | Uses what is specific here: a CEO retiring at close, 95 percent market overlap, dual HQs, a rebrand at closing, an AI and centralization thesis stated on day one | "Mergers create integration challenges" (true of any merger, fails) |
| Q5 Number at risk | The $125M net synergy commitment, or the margin expansion thesis, as the thing analysts will score | No number anywhere |
| Swap test | No sentence survives a Public Storage / NSA swap | Any surviving sentence |
| Hygiene A | [VERIFY] on any Symposium content claim | Asserted track or session nobody verified |
| Hygiene B | Names or [CONFIRM]s the tech leader; never asks Schall for a routing intro | "Could you point me to whoever leads IT?" |
| Tiers | Claims map to fact base rows; inference framed as observation | Untiered filler like "Gartner is seeing leading CIOs..." |
| Format | 75 to 100 words, no em dashes | Outside range or em dashes present |

## Facilitator answer key: what a strong Q1 to Q5 looks like for this scenario

- Q1: All-stock merger of equals announced 5/21/26.
- Q2: Which means their technology function is likely dealing with consolidating two property management stacks, two resident-facing platforms, and two data environments, under a synergy clock, while 10,800 units are under construction.
- Q3: Hard. Neither company has integrated a peer of its own size; this is a merger of equals with dual HQs and a rebrand, not a bolt-on. (Have reps verify integration history rather than assert it.)
- Q4: Instance-specific angles: one of the two CEOs retires at close, so the tech integration outlasts half the leadership that announced it; 95 percent market overlap means consolidation, not coexistence; the AI, automation, and centralized services thesis was stated on day one, so technology IS the synergy plan, not a supporting function.
- Q5: The $125M net synergy number, which analysts and both shareholder bases will score quarterly. The margin expansion promise depends on the technology consolidation going well.

## North-star sample email (share only AFTER Round 2)

"Ben, the $125 million net synergy number you committed to on May 21 gets scored against two property management stacks, two resident platforms, and one day-one promise of AI-driven margin expansion. With 95 percent market overlap, every duplicated system is now a line item analysts will track quarterly. Sequencing that consolidation is what Symposium pressure-tests in October. [VERIFY: relevant track] I'd want [CONFIRM: combined company tech integration lead] in the room while the operating model is still being decided."

(79 words. Every claim maps to fact base rows 4, 7, and 8, with Tier 3 inference framed as observation. Placeholders model the hygiene standard.)

## Objection playbook for the room

Expect the same objections the rep simulator throws. Responses that work, in order of use:

- "My Gem emails work fine." Concede first: "They fixed volume and formatting, and that was real. The question is what happens to reply rates when every competitor's rep sends the same email. That decay shows up slowly, then all at once."
- "I have 300 accounts, the math doesn't work." Honor the constraint: "Run the Five Questions on your top 20. Keep volume plays for the long tail. Nobody is asking for 300 worksheets."
- "Where's the evidence?" Point at the screen: "You just read twelve drafts of the same email. That is the evidence. The market did the A/B test for us."
- "That's analyst work, not BDR work." Career framing: "Reading a press release and asking what it forces operationally is the skill that makes you the rep a CIO takes a meeting with. It is also the skill AI cannot fake, which makes it the one that keeps you paid."
- Pleasant agreement with no commitment ("sure, I'll try it"). Do not accept it. Ask for something concrete and dated: "Great. Pick one account, bring the worksheet Thursday, and we'll score the draft together."

## After the workshop

- Roll out the Email Scorer prompt as the standing gate: no first-touch email to a top-20 account goes out without a scorecard.
- Rerun the side-by-side in 60 days with a fresh headline. Same anonymity, same questions. Improvement should be visible in how few sentences survive the swap test.
