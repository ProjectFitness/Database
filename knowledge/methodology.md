# Sales Methodology Reference

How the agent evaluates and generates discovery questions. Blend of **Gap Selling** (Keenan) and **The Challenger Sale** (Dixon/Adamson), adapted for selling Gartner research & advisory to technology leaders.

## The non-negotiable anchor: the quantified corporate objective

Every discovery thread must eventually trace to a **corporate objective** that is:

1. **Quantified** — a number, not a vibe ("grow total assets from $4.2B to $6B", not "grow")
2. **Time-bound** — a deadline or horizon ("by end of FY27")
3. **Owned above the CIO** — set by the CEO/board/ELT, because CIOs/CISOs at sub-$1B firms rarely have a seat at the table; their initiatives only get funded when tied to what the business already committed to
4. **Connected to technology** — an explicit line from the tech initiative to the objective ("inorganic growth means M&A; M&A means core conversion and day-one integration; that's where modernization contributes")

If a discovery conversation ends without all four, the discovery is incomplete. The agent should always flag this.

## Gap Selling structure

Discovery maps the gap between current state and future state. The gap is the value; no gap, no deal.

| Element | What to uncover | Example questions |
|---|---|---|
| **Current state (facts)** | Literal situation: team size, stack, vendors, budget, in-flight initiatives | "Walk me through how decisions on the core platform get made today." |
| **Current state (problems)** | What's broken, slow, risky, expensive | "Where does that process break down?" |
| **Root cause** | Why the problem exists — keep asking why until you hit cause, not symptom | "What's driven the three failed attempts to fix this?" |
| **Impact (quantified)** | What the problem costs in dollars, time, risk, credibility | "What does a six-month delay on the conversion do to the integration savings the board was promised?" |
| **Future state** | What good looks like, in their words, with numbers | "If this worked, what would the board see differently in 18 months?" |
| **Cost of inaction (COI)** | What happens if nothing changes — the deal-mover | "If this stays as-is through next year's exam cycle, what happens?" |
| **Emotional/political stake** | What it means for *them* personally | "What does the ELT conclude about the IT org if this slips again?" |

### Question laddering (1st → 2nd → 3rd level)

- **Level 1 (surface):** "What are your priorities this year?" — anyone can ask this; answers are safe and rehearsed.
- **Level 2 (mechanism):** "Why is that a priority *now*? Who set it? What changed?" — uncovers the driver and the political map.
- **Level 3 (consequence & quantification):** "What's it worth? What breaks if it slips? How will the CEO measure whether it worked? What's your credibility exposure on it?" — this is where the corporate objective, timeline, and COI live.

The coach should never accept a Level 1 answer as discovery. Every priority named gets laddered: **driver → owner → number → timeline → consequence → personal stake.**

## Challenger elements

- **Teach (commercial insight):** Lead with what peers in their sub-vertical are doing — reframe their view of the problem. For Gartner sellers this is natural: cite the critical-priority patterns (see `priorities.md`) and what same-size FS peers struggle with.
- **Tailor:** Frame everything in the metrics of *their* seat (CIO: run-cost ratio, project delivery credibility; CISO: exam findings, board reporting; CDAO: data products tied to revenue).
- **Take control:** Constructive tension is good. Don't retreat at the first "we're all set." Push back on vague answers, name the elephant ("most CIOs your size tell me the AI mandate came down without budget — is that what's happening here?"), and always exit with a concrete, calendared next step with a named additional stakeholder where possible.

## Gartner-specific value frame

Gartner doesn't execute — it advises. So the value case is always:

- **De-risk decisions:** the cost of a wrong vendor pick / failed program / bad architecture bet dwarfs the contract value
- **Accelerate:** compress months of internal analysis into weeks (time-to-decision as money)
- **Arm the leader:** give the CIO/CISO the board-grade narrative and peer benchmarking they need to *get* the seat at the table
- **Cost of inaction framing:** "what does deciding this without independent validation cost you if it goes wrong?"

Discovery should therefore probe **decisions on the calendar** (vendor selections, board presentations, budget cycles, exams, M&A diligence) — Gartner sells best against a dated decision.

## Anti-patterns the coach must call out

- Accepting a critical priority ("we're modernizing apps") with no number, owner, or date attached
- Feature-dumping Gartner deliverables before the gap is established
- Solving (pitching) at the first pain mentioned instead of finding root cause
- Question stacking (asking three questions at once)
- Happy ears: treating polite interest as a buying signal
- Closing for "a follow-up call" instead of a next step with new stakeholders or new information committed
- Letting the prospect interview the seller ("send me what you cover in that area")
