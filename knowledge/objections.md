# Objection & Deflection Bank

Used by `/roleplay` (personas deploy these) and `/coach` (drill responses to them). Each entry: the objection, what's usually *really* behind it, and what a strong handling looks like — so the coach can grade attempts honestly instead of accepting any rebuttal.

## Deflections (early-cycle, designed to end the conversation politely)

| Deflection | What's usually behind it | Strong handling looks like |
|---|---|---|
| "Just send me some information / pricing." | No perceived gap yet; wants to exit politely. | Don't comply or refuse — trade: name a specific peer insight, ask one sharp question to test for a real initiative; pricing without scope is meaningless and says so respectfully. |
| "We're all set / no budget this year." | Budget exists for things tied to corporate objectives; *this* isn't tied to one yet. | Ignore budget, hunt the dated decision: "Most clients don't buy from budget — they buy against a decision. What's the biggest tech decision on your calendar in the next two quarters?" |
| "Call me back in Q4 / after the conversion." | The conversion IS the pain window — afterwards the need is gone. | Reframe: the highest-stakes decisions are happening *now*; waiting means deciding the big stuff unadvised. |
| "I need to loop in my team first." | Either a brush-off or a real power map signal. | Welcome it AND qualify it: who specifically, what do they own, and book the date now. |
| "What does Gartner even do? You don't implement anything." | Legitimate confusion + a test. | Don't get defensive. Reframe to decision risk: implementers are conflicted on the decisions that precede implementation; Gartner sits on the buyer's side of the table. Then back to discovery. |

## Competitive / substitute objections

| Objection | What's usually behind it | Strong handling looks like |
|---|---|---|
| "We already have Forrester / IDC / a smaller advisory." | Sometimes true, often a shield; usage is typically shallow (one license, rarely used). | Discover, don't bash: who uses it, for what decisions, last time it changed an outcome. Sell the unused-seat problem if it surfaces. |
| "Our consultants / auditors / Big 4 handle that." | Conflates execution advice with independent advice. | Tension point: their consultants profit from the answer being "do a big project with us." Who validates the validator? |
| "I can find this on Google / ChatGPT for free." | Price anchoring + genuine generational objection, very common with AI-mandate personas. | Don't dismiss it — concede what's true, then differentiate: peer-validated data, analyst access for *their* specific decision, and accountability; free advice has no skin in the game. |
| "We lean on our peer network / CUSO / banker's association." | Real and valuable, especially credit unions. | Respect it, then probe its limits: did the peer network price their last core renewal, or benchmark their security spend? |

## Price / value objections

| Objection | What's usually behind it | Strong handling looks like |
|---|---|---|
| "That's expensive for a subscription." | No gap established — price is only high relative to zero value. | Return to COI: anchor against the decision at stake (a mis-negotiated core contract, a failed program, a repeat exam finding), not against "content." |
| "A colleague had Gartner and never used it." | Real risk; deserves honesty. | Agree it happens, explain why (bought as a library, not tied to decisions), and tie *this* proposal to their named decisions and dates. |
| "I can't get this approved — I don't control that spend." | The seat-at-the-table problem, stated plainly. The most honest objection in the book. | This is the wedge, not the wall: whose objective does the initiative serve? Build the business case in the CFO/CEO's language together — that's literally the service. |

## Mid-conversation pressure moves (hostile mode)

- "You've asked me a lot of questions — what do I get out of this call?" *(seller must deliver insight on demand)*
- "We took this call as a courtesy to [colleague]. I've got 10 more minutes." *(tests prioritization under time pressure)*
- "Everything you've said, our incumbent already gives us." *(tests differentiation without bashing)*
- "Pitch me right now: why should a $700M bank pay for this?" *(tests whether seller pitches prematurely or bridges back to *their* situation)*
- "My CFO will laugh at this. He thinks analysts are astrology for executives." *(tests composure + business-case framing)*
