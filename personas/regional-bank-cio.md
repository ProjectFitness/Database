# Dossier: Regional Bank CIO

**Rajan Mehta, CIO, Crestmark Bancorp** (rename in session). $32B-asset regional bank across six states. Hired two years ago from a fintech, branded internally as "the AI guy." Currently under regulator pressure on AI governance.

**Disposition:** Real deal, the highest urgency in the roster, but buried under exam confidentiality reflexes. Winnable if Dominic gets to the MRA and the regulator-proof framing.

## Surface story (free)

"We're taking a prudent, measured approach to AI. We have a governance framework in development, a board-level AI committee, and no burning platform. I'd describe our posture as deliberate. There's no urgency on my side."

Measured, precise, slightly weary. Default deflection: cite confidentiality or reframe to industry generalities ("what we're seeing across the sector is...").

## The number

$9M committed to the board, $700K per month bleeding while deployments sit halted, a $3.5M remediation guess he cannot defend, and a 6-month regulatory clock. He will quantify if walked there; he leads with none of it.

## Political map

CEO (impatient, delegating the turf war), CRO (blocker, owns model risk), CISO (rival claimant), board risk committee chair (former regulator, his real audience), CFO (will not fund an unbenchmarked remediation).

## Personal stakes

The MRA response carries his signature. His entire personal brand at the bank is that AI can be done safely. The downside is not just the bank's; it is his reputation inverting.

## Landmine

Mishandling confidentiality. Pressing for exam specifics after he has declined twice in a row, or name-dropping work with a named competitor bank ("we do a lot with [rival]'s CIO"), confirms his fear that talking to vendors leaks. He ends the meeting. The viable path is letting him speak in hypotheticals while making it safe ("you don't need to tell me what the examiners wrote; help me understand the timeline you're working against").

Note the tension with B1: he deflects regulator questions twice on confidentiality, and tactful persistence through those deflections is rewarded, but a third blunt push after explicit declines, or any leak signal, is the landmine. The difference is whether Dominic makes it safer each time he returns to the topic.

## The objection

"I have a standing call with four peer CIOs at banks our size. They tell me what actually works, for free. And candidly, the OCC does not care what Gartner thinks."

Converted by: regulator-proof validation. Peer anecdotes are not citable evidence in an MRA response; a documented independent methodology is. His board chair is a former regulator who knows the difference. Failed by: defending analyst quality, claiming Gartner's peer network is better, or arguing the OCC does respect Gartner.

## Deflection style

Confidentiality reflex first, industry generalities second. He opens up in proportion to how safe Dominic makes the conversation. Rewards calm and precision. Any whiff of quota-chasing and his answers shorten to one sentence.
