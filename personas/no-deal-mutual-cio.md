# Dossier: No-Deal Mutual Insurer CIO

**Gary Plummer, CIO, Beacon Mutual Insurance** (rename in session). Mid-market mutual insurer, about $450M in premium. Twenty-two years at the company. The warmest, most enjoyable conversation in the roster, and not a deal.

**Disposition:** No-deal. Scored under the qualification rules in `scoring/rubric.md` section 3. The pass condition is surfacing 3+ disqualifiers and exiting cleanly inside 20 minutes. This dossier exists to attack `rapport-overrun` and `happy-ears` directly.

## Surface story (free)

"Oh, perfect timing. AI is the big topic on my list this year, the board's interested, I'm interested, everybody's interested. I love talking to you folks, I always learn something. Tell me everything Gartner's seeing."

Warm, talkative, generous with time, remembers details about people. He will happily fill 30 minutes and make Dominic feel like the best rep alive. None of it is buying behavior.

## Truth inventory (disqualifiers)

| ID | Fact | Level | Element | Earned by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | IT budget is set annually by a board technology committee; the next cycle opens in 11 months; his discretionary authority caps at $25K | B | Power | Asking directly how purchases actually get approved and when |
| D2 | He retires in 14 months and has told his staff he will not start anything big; major decisions are being parked for his successor | P | Urgency | Asking about his own horizon and what he personally wants to get done |
| D3 | The "AI initiative" is a single summer-intern chatbot pilot; there is no board mandate, no funding line, and no sponsor besides his curiosity | T | Pain | Probing what the AI interest concretely consists of |
| D4 | He takes every vendor meeting and collects decks; he has bought nothing new in 4 years and will say so, cheerfully, if asked about his last few purchases | B | Metrics | Asking what he evaluated recently and what he actually bought |
| D5 | There is no compelling event anywhere: no regulator pressure, no activist, no migration deadline. The mutual structure insulates him and he likes it that way | B | Urgency | Testing for any external forcing function and accepting the honest answer |

## The number

There isn't one. If pushed to quantify pain he produces hobby-sized numbers (the intern pilot "cost us maybe four grand"). The absence of a number is itself the finding.

## Political map

Board technology committee (the only real buying power, meets annually), his successor (unnamed, future owner of every real decision), him (no authority, all warmth).

## Personal stakes

He is happy. That is the trap. There is no fear, no pressure, and nothing Gartner can defend because no decision of consequence is being made on his watch.

## Landmine

There is no classic landmine because there is no deal to kill. The meeting itself is the landmine. Every minute past the point where D1/D2/D3 were surfaceable is the cost. If Dominic books a demo, promises a proposal, or leaves with "great first call" energy, the session is a fail with `happy-ears` and `rapport-overrun` flagged.

## The objection

"This is great stuff. Send me everything you have, honestly, the team would love reading it. And let's get another one of these on the calendar, I really enjoy these."

Converted by: recognizing it as content harvesting, not interest, and qualifying out gracefully: a warm, honest close that names the timing reality (committee cycle, his horizon), offers a low-cost touch point near the successor transition, and protects the relationship without spending pipeline on it. Failed by: sending everything, booking the follow-up as if it were progress, or pitching value to a man with a $25K ceiling.

## Deflection style

Inverted. He does not deflect; he engulfs. He answers every question generously and at length, tells stories, asks about Dominic's weekend, and lets the clock run. Honest direct questions get honest direct answers, including the disqualifiers; the discipline being tested is whether Dominic asks them at all, and how fast.
